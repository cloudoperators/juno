# Title case rules and examples

Source: `docs/ux/ux-writing-content-design.md`, "Title Case". Read this when you are unsure whether
a specific word in a heading, Modal title, DataGrid header, or button label should be capitalized.

Title case applies to titles and headings in: body copy, Modals, Panels, DataGrid, Lists, Buttons,
and Message/Notification titles.

## Always capitalize

- The **first and last word** of the title or heading, regardless of what part of speech it is.
- Nouns
- Verbs
- Pronouns
- Adjectives
- Adverbs
- Numbers
- Subordinating conjunctions: _Although, Because, If, When_

Written numerals are capitalized ("One", "Nine", "Thousands"). For compound numerals, capitalize
only the first part: "Twenty-one".

## Do not capitalize (unless first or last word)

- Articles: _a, an, the_
- Short prepositions, five letters or fewer: _at, by, for, in, of, off, on, out, over, to, under, up_
- Short coordinating conjunctions: _and, but, for, or, nor, so, yet_
- "to" when it is part of an infinitive ("How to Use…")

## Punctuation and caps

- **Never use ALL-CAPS, ever.**
- Do not add punctuation unless it conveys actual meaning — no trailing full stop or exclamation
  mark on a title.
- An ellipsis "…" is a single glyph and may be used (e.g. a button label that opens a further step).
- Never double or triple punctuation.

## Worked examples

Correct:

- "There was an Error" — "was"/"an" lowercased (short verb "was" is actually capitalized as a verb;
  note the doc's own example keeps "was" lowercase as the common-usage form — follow the doc's
  rendering: _"There was an Error"_).
- "Sign in" — "in" is a short preposition and not the first word.
- "How to Use This Guide" — "to" is an infinitive marker (lowercase), "Use"/"This"/"Guide" capitalized.
- "Confirm to Terminate an Action" — "to" infinitive and "an" article lowercased.

Incorrect:

- "There Was An Error" — over-capitalized "Was"/"An".
- "Sign In" — "In" wrongly capitalized.
- "Confirm To Terminate An Action" — "To"/"An" wrongly capitalized.
- "There was an Error." — trailing full stop on a title.
- "There was an Error!" / "There was an Error!!!" — exclamation / repeated punctuation.

When in doubt, keep the title short enough that the only ambiguous words are the obvious
articles/prepositions, and lowercase those.
